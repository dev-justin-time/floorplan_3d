Build a stair you can climb comfortably

The vocabulary, the ten formulas and the worked example below follow the method behind the straight stair designer and the editor's stair engine. Everything is metric; the imperial equivalents are the published US ranges.

Governing comfort rules: G + 2H 590–650 mm · going 230–330 mm · step height 150–200 mm · slope 20–50° · headroom ≥ 1900 mm · width > 700 mm.

Formulas

H = height · h = step height · G = going · B = Blondel value · θ = angle · d = step depth. The example column uses the 3000 mm worked example below.

QuantityFormulaExample

Ranges: step height 150–200 mm (ideal 175), going 230–330 mm (ideal 280), Blondel 590–650 mm (ideal 630), slope 20–50° (ideal 30–35°), headroom ≥ 1900 mm (ideal ≥ 2100), width > 700 mm (ideal ≥ 800). When the space is fixed, solve by length instead: G = (L − landing step) ÷ steps.

  1. 1 · Number of steps. Divide the height by the ideal 175 mm step height, round, then subtract one. steps = round(3000 ÷ 175) − 1 = round(17.14) − 1 = 16 · risers = 17
  2. 2 · Step height. Divide the height by the number of risers. h = 3000 ÷ (16 + 1) = 3000 ÷ 17 = 176.47 mm
  3. 3 · Going. Blondel's law, rearranged. G = 630 − 2 × 176.47 = 277.06 mm
  4. 4 · Step depth and overlap. Add a 45 mm overlap, round the depth to the nearest 10 mm, then take the overlap back out. d = round(277.06 + 45) = 322.06 → 320 mm · overlap = 320 − 277.06 = 42.94 mm
  5. 5 · Landing step. Optional step in the extension of the upper floor; choose it slightly larger than the overlap. landing step ≥ 42.94 → 50 mm (next 10 mm)
  6. 6 · Angle. Trigonometry, step height over going. θ = arctan(176.47 ÷ 277.06) = 32.49°
  7. 7 · Total run. Every going, plus the landing step. L = 16 × 277.06 + 50 = 4482.96 mm
  8. 8 · Headroom. Measure at the ceiling edge, back from the top nosing. headroom = tan(32.49°) × (3500 − 50) − 250 = 1947.45 mm

The designer keeps the step depth exact (going + overlap) and takes the overlap as an input, so it can differ by a few millimetres from the hand-rounded example without changing the checks.

Adjustments

Change the step count or the Blondel value and re-run every formula. Fewer steps or a lower Blondel value shortens the run and raises the headroom; more steps or a higher Blondel value lengthen the run and eat the headroom.

Angle bands

A stair sits between 20° and 50°; steeper is a ladder, shallower is a ramp.
