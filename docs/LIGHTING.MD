# Lighting and power plan · rules and regulations

The lighting suite designs luminaires, switches, sockets, branch circuits and a
costed takeoff. This document is the reference behind the plan checks: the 2021
International Residential Code (IRC), which adopts the 2020 National Electrical
Code (NEC) for electrical provisions, and the 2021 International Energy
Conservation Code (IECC).

> **This is a design aid, not a permit drawing.** Codes are adopted and amended
> locally, and the authority having jurisdiction (AHJ) decides. Confirm the
> edition in force, then have a licensed electrician review the final plan and
> perform the work. The suite's prices are planning allowances, not quotes.

| Concern | Model code | What it controls |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Lighting outlets and switching | IRC 2021 Chapter 39, §E3903 | Where a light is required and how it is controlled |
| Receptacle spacing | IRC 2021 §E3901 | Wall, counter, floor and special-room outlets |
| GFCI protection | IRC 2021 §E3902.1–E3902.10 | Wet, damp and appliance locations |
| AFCI protection | IRC 2021 §E3902.20 | 120 V, 15/20 A branch circuits |
| Boxes, cable and overcurrent | IRC 2021 §E3905 | Box fill, support, circuit rating |
| Working clearance | IRC 2021 §E3405 / NEC 110.26 | Clear space at panels |
| Fixture ratings | IRC 2021 §E3904 / NEC 410 | Damp, wet, IC and fire-rated luminaires |

The IRC's electrical chapter is a residential extract of the NEC. Where the two
differ in wording, the adopted edition and local amendment govern. Commercial or
mixed-use work follows the NEC and the commercial energy code, not this page.

## 2. Lighting outlets and switching

### 2.1 Required lighting outlets

**IRC §E3903.2.** At least one lighting outlet controlled by a listed
wall-mounted control device is required in every habitable room, kitchen and
bathroom.

- The *lighting outlet* is the box and circuit, not the luminaire. The fixture
  is selected later.
- The control must be wall mounted and listed. A pull chain, a lamp switch or a
  remote-only control does not satisfy the requirement.
- A switched receptacle is not a universal substitute. Some editions and rooms
  permit it; check the local amendment and the room.

**IRC §E3903.3 / NEC 210.70(A)(2).** Additional locations:

| Location | Requirement |
| --- | --- |
| Hallway | At least one wall-switch-controlled lighting outlet |
| Stairway | At least one; with six or more risers, a switch at the top and bottom |
| Attached garage | At least one switched lighting outlet, independent of the door operator |
| Outdoor entrance | At least one switched lighting outlet at an outdoor entrance |
| Storage room | A lighting outlet where the room is used for storage |

### 2.2 Switch placement

The code requires a wall control; good practice puts it where a person enters:

- 1.0–1.5 m from the door opening, 1.2 m above the floor.
- On the latch side, clear of the door swing and the trim.
- Not inside a closet or behind a door that opens against it.
- A dimmer or sensor still needs a manual control unless a specific exception
  applies.

The suite warns when the nearest switch is more than 1.6 m from the plan's entry
point. Move it before the cable is pulled; moving a switch after drywall is a
repair, not a design change.

### 2.3 Switching arrangements

| Arrangement | Use | Conductors |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Single-pole | One location controls the load | 2-wire switch loop or 2-wire feed |
| Three-way | Two locations control the same load | Two travellers plus common at each end |
| Four-way | A third or fourth control point between two three-ways | Four travellers between the three-ways |
| Dimmer | Manual dimming; may be smart | Match the dimmer to the LED driver and its rating |
| Occupancy sensor | Automatic on, automatic off | Usually needs a neutral; check the load type |
| Vacancy sensor | Manual on, automatic off | Preferred where false-on is annoying |

A switch loop has no neutral at the switch. Most smart dimmers and sensors need
one, so pull a three-conductor cable if an upgrade is possible.

### 2.4 Layered lighting

One ceiling fixture for everything is why rooms feel flat. A workable
residential balance:

| Layer | Share of installed lumens | Typical fittings |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ambient | 55–70% | Recessed downlights, flush mount, linear |
| Task | 20–30% | Under-cabinet strip, vanity bar, desk lamp |
| Accent | 10–20% | Wall wash, gimbal, art light, step light |
| Decor | 0–10% | Pendant, chandelier, decorative globe |

Switch and dim each layer separately. The suite reports the layer share so an
all-ambient plan is visible before it is built.

## 3. Receptacles

### 3.1 The 6 ft / 12 ft rule

**IRC §E3901.2.1 (spacing) and §E3901.2.2 (wall space).** In every kitchen,
family room, dining room, living room, parlor, library, den, sunroom, bedroom,
recreation room, or similar room, no point along the floor line of a wall space
may be more than 6 ft (1.83 m) from a receptacle. In practice receptacles are
placed no more than 12 ft (3.66 m) apart.

- Any wall space 2 ft (610 mm) or wider counts, including the segment between a
  door and an adjoining wall.
- A floor receptacle counts only if it is within 18 in (457 mm) of the wall.
- Fixed room dividers and sliding panels create separate wall spaces.
- The rule is about the floor line, so a high mounted receptacle does not
  necessarily satisfy a wall space unless the code allows it.

The suite's default spacing is 3.66 m. That puts a socket within 1.83 m of each
corner and no more than 3.66 m between sockets. A 1.83 m kitchen spacing is a
planning approximation of the counter rule, not the rule itself.

### 3.2 Room-by-room receptacle requirements

| Room | Requirement | Protection |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Living, bedroom, dining, family | 6 ft / 12 ft wall spacing | AFCI for the branch circuit |
| Kitchen counter | Every counter space 12 in or wider; no point more than 24 in from a receptacle; not more than 12 in below the counter | GFCI, two 20 A small-appliance circuits |
| Bathroom | At least one receptacle within 36 in of the basin; none in a tub or shower space | GFCI |
| Laundry | At least one 20 A laundry circuit | GFCI |
| Garage | At least one receptacle | GFCI |
| Outdoor | At least one at the front and one at the back of a one-family dwelling, not more than 6.5 ft above grade | GFCI, WR, in-use cover |
| Hallway | Receptacles where the wall space rule applies; lighting is the primary requirement | AFCI |

### 3.3 Kitchen and countertop rules

**IRC §E3901.3.** At least two 20 A small-appliance branch circuits supply the
kitchen countertop receptacles.

- 20 A, 12 AWG copper, GFCI protected.
- No lighting outlets and no other general receptacles on the small-appliance
  circuits.
- A dining room, breakfast room or pantry receptacle may be on either circuit.
- A dedicated appliance (range, dishwasher, disposal, microwave) is on its own
  circuit, sized from the nameplate.

**IRC §E3901.4.** Countertop receptacles:

- Every wall countertop space 12 in (305 mm) or wider needs a receptacle.
- No point along the counter may be more than 24 in (610 mm) from a receptacle.
- Not more than 12 in (305 mm) below the countertop.
- Not behind a range, refrigerator or sink; split the counter at the appliance.
- Islands and peninsulas need at least one; small islands have limited
  exceptions. Check the current edition.

### 3.4 Bathroom

**IRC §E3901.6, §E3902.1.** A bathroom needs:

- At least one GFCI-protected receptacle within 36 in (914 mm) of the outside
  edge of the basin.
- No receptacle inside a tub or shower space, including a compartment.
- A damp-rated luminaire; a fixture over a tub or shower must be wet rated and
  listed for the application.

Light the face from both sides of the mirror rather than straight down. A
single vanity bar above the mirror leaves shadows under the eyes.

### 3.5 Laundry, garage and outdoor

**Laundry.** At least one 20 A laundry branch circuit, GFCI protected, with no
other outlets except limited allowances for the laundry light or exhaust fan. A
gas dryer still needs a 120 V receptacle; an electric dryer needs a dedicated
30 A, 240 V circuit.

**Garage.** At least one receptacle, GFCI protected, and at least one switched
light independent of the door operator. Keep the panel clear of stored goods and
protect it from vehicle impact.

**Outdoor.** At least one receptacle at the front and one at the back of a
one-family dwelling, not more than 6.5 ft (1.98 m) above grade. Use a
weather-resistant receptacle, an in-use cover and GFCI protection. Outdoor
luminaires need a wet-location listing; a damp-rated fixture is not enough.

## 4. GFCI and AFCI

### 4.1 GFCI locations

**IRC §E3902 / NEC 210.8.** Ground-fault circuit-interrupter protection is
required for 125 V, single-phase, 15/20 A receptacles in:

| Section | Location |
| --- | --- |
| E3902.1 | Bathrooms |
| E3902.2 | Garages and accessory buildings |
| E3902.3 | Outdoors |
| E3902.4 | Crawlspaces |
| E3902.5 | Unfinished basements |
| E3902.6 | Kitchen countertop surfaces |
| E3902.7 | Laundry, utility and bar sinks |
| E3902.8 | Boathouses |
| E3902.9 | Bathtub and shower spaces |
| E3902.10 | Other locations within 6 ft of a sink, tub or shower |

GFCI protection may be at the breaker or at the first receptacle. Downstream
receptacles are protected only when the line/load terminals are wired correctly.
GFCI does not replace the equipment ground and does not protect against a short
circuit or overload.

### 4.2 AFCI

**IRC §E3902.20 / NEC 210.12.** Most 120 V, single-phase, 15 and 20 A branch
circuits supplying outlets in a dwelling need combination-type AFCI protection.

- Use a combination-type AFCI breaker at the origin of the branch circuit.
- An outlet-branch AFCI is an alternative only where the code permits it.
- Label the panel; an AFCI trip is a diagnostic, not a nuisance to bypass.
- AFCI and GFCI can be combined in one breaker where both are required.

The suite records AFCI as an advisory because the breaker type is chosen at the
panel. Mark every 120 V branch as AFCI on the panel schedule.

## 5. Circuits, cable and boxes

### 5.1 Branch-circuit ratings

| Circuit | Breaker | Copper | Typical load |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Lighting | 15 A | 14 AWG | 12 A continuous maximum |
| General receptacles | 20 A | 12 AWG | 16 A continuous maximum |
| Kitchen small appliance | 20 A | 12 AWG | Two or more circuits |
| Laundry | 20 A | 12 AWG | One circuit |
| Bathroom | 20 A | 12 AWG | GFCI, vanity and general |
| Dryer | 30 A, two-pole | 10 AWG | Nameplate |
| Range | 50 A, two-pole | 6 AWG | Nameplate |

**IRC §E3905 / NEC 210.19–210.23.** The connected load must stay within the
branch-circuit rating. Continuous loads are limited to 80% of the breaker. The
suite warns above 80% and fails above 100%.

For load calculation:

- A general receptacle counts 180 VA each.
- A luminaire counts its fixture wattage.
- A dedicated appliance counts its nameplate rating.
- The service calculation adds the range, dryer, water heater and EV charger;
  the suite does not perform a service calculation.

### 5.2 Cable and support

- Support cable within 12 in (305 mm) of a box and every 4.5 ft (1.37 m).
- Protect cable where it passes through framing; use a listed bushing or plate.
- Do not strip more sheath than needed inside the box.
- Use a listed connector at every box entry.
- Keep power and low-voltage cable separated; do not share a knockout without a
  listed divider.

### 5.3 Box fill

**IRC §E3905.12 / NEC 314.16.** Every box needs enough cubic-inch capacity for
its conductors, clamps, grounding and device.

Count:

- Each current-carrying conductor.
- The grounded (neutral) conductor.
- The equipment grounding conductor.
- One allowance for the device or equipment.
- One allowance for internal clamps.
- A support fitting may add an allowance.

A 2-1/8 in deep single-gang box is not automatically large enough for two 12/2
cables plus a smart dimmer. Check the fill calculation on crowded switch boxes;
smart devices have a large body.

## 6. Luminaire ratings and locations

### 6.1 Damp and wet locations

**IRC §E3904 / NEC 410.10.**

- **Damp location:** protected from weather but possibly wet — bathrooms, covered
  porches, laundry areas. Use a damp-rated luminaire.
- **Wet location:** exposed to weather — an outdoor wall, a post, an uncovered
  entry. Use a wet-rated luminaire and a gasketed, listed box.
- A luminaire over a tub or shower must be wet rated and listed for the
  application.
- Keep the driver and connections inside the listed enclosure, not in the wall
  cavity.

### 6.2 Insulation contact and fire rating

**IRC §E3904 / NEC 410.116.**

- A recessed luminaire in an insulated ceiling must be IC rated (insulation
  contact) and should be airtight.
- A non-IC can needs a clearance box or the insulation kept clear; the
  manufacturer's instructions govern.
- A fire-resistance-rated ceiling needs a fire-rated luminaire or a listed
  protection of the ceiling membrane.
- Do not bury a driver or junction box under insulation unless it is listed for
  it.

### 6.3 Mounting heights (design practice)

| Device | Typical height |
| --- | --- |
| General switch | 1.2 m (48 in) |
| General receptacle | 0.3 m (12 in) |
| Kitchen counter receptacle | 1.1 m (42–48 in) |
| Bathroom vanity receptacle | 1.1 m (42–48 in) |
| Bedside receptacle | 1.1 m (42–48 in) |
| Wall sconce | 1.7–1.8 m to centre |
| Recessed downlight | ceiling less 0–150 mm |
| Pendant over a table | 750–900 mm above the top |

Heights are not all code requirements. The code sets some locations (for
example, the countertop receptacle not more than 12 in below the counter); the
rest are ergonomics.

## 7. Energy code

### 7.1 High-efficacy sources

**IECC 2021 §R404.1 / IRC §N1104.1.** Permanently installed luminaires must
contain only high-efficacy sources, in practice LED at 65 lm/W or better.

- A screw-in lamp in a permanent fixture counts; choose the lamp as carefully as
  the fixture.
- Kitchen appliance lighting and some specialty fixtures have limited
  exceptions.
- Keep the cut sheet to prove efficacy, CCT and CRI at inspection.

### 7.2 Lighting controls

**IECC 2021 §R404.2 / IRC §N1104.2.** Permanently installed lighting in
habitable spaces must be controlled by a dimmer, an occupant sensor or an
equivalent control built into the fixture.

- Applies to living, sleeping, eating and cooking spaces.
- A simple on/off switch may not satisfy the control requirement.
- Commission the dimmer with the actual LED load; check for flicker and buzz.
- A smart dimmer may need a neutral at the switch box.

### 7.3 Lighting power density

The suite reports installed W/m² as a planning metric. Residential energy codes
usually regulate the whole-home lighting power rather than a room-by-room cap,
so use the number to compare layouts, not as a pass/fail limit. A layered LED
design typically lands under 12–16 W/m².

## 8. Photometry used by the suite

### 8.1 Lumen method

Average maintained illuminance at the work plane:

```
E_avg = (N × Φ × CU × LLF) / A
```

| Symbol | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| N | Number of luminaires |
| Φ | Lumens per luminaire |
| CU | Coefficient of utilization |
| LLF | Light loss factor (0.80 default) |
| A | Room area (m²) |

Room index:

```
K = (L × W) / (Hm × (L + W))
```

`Hm` is the mounting height above the work plane. The suite derives CU from the
room index and surface reflectances, clamped to a realistic range.

### 8.2 Point-by-point grid

The canvas heatmap samples a 20 × 20 grid at the work plane (0.75 m). Each
luminaire is a point source with intensity `Φ / π`; illuminance falls with the
cosine of the angle and the square of the distance. The grid gives:

- Minimum and maximum illuminance.
- Uniformity `E_min / E_max`.
- Average-to-minimum ratio.

### 8.3 Targets

| Room | Target average | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Living | 200 lx | Layer task and accent; dim ambient |
| Bedroom | 150 lx | Warm 2700 K, low glare |
| Kitchen | 300 lx | 3500 K; under-cabinet task at the counter |
| Bathroom | 250 lx | Light the face from both sides |
| Dining | 200 lx | Pendant 750–900 mm above the table |
| Hallway | 100 lx | Three-way control on a long run |
| Stairway | 150 lx | Switch at each floor level |
| Home office | 400 lx | Neutral CCT, task light at the desk |
| Laundry | 250 lx | Light into the appliances |
| Garage | 250 lx | Shop light over the bench |
| Closet | 150 lx | Keep the fixture clear of shelving |
| Outdoor entry | 100 lx | Wet-rated, glare controlled |

These are design targets, not code minima. Uniformity of 0.40 or better is a
useful comfort target; a high max-to-average ratio suggests glare or scalloping.

### 8.4 Colour and glare

- CRI 90+ where people eat, work or get ready; ask for R9 on wood and skin.
- One CCT per room; a 2700 K lamp beside a 4000 K lamp reads yellow and blue at
  once.
- A narrow beam needs closer spacing and leaves scallops on a wall.
- Deep regress, shields and dimming control glare; a bare lamp in the view is a
  design failure even when the average lux is correct.

## 11. Inspection checklist

### Plan review

- [ ] Room dimensions and ceiling height match the architectural plan.
- [ ] Every habitable room, kitchen, bathroom, hallway, stair, garage and
  outdoor entrance has the required lighting outlet.
- [ ] Every light is controlled by a wall switch, sensor or listed control.
- [ ] Stairs have a switch at each floor level; long halls have two.
- [ ] Receptacle spacing satisfies 6 ft / 12 ft, including 2 ft wall segments.
- [ ] Kitchen counters have receptacles in every 12 in+ space, within 24 in.
- [ ] Two 20 A small-appliance circuits; no lighting on them.
- [ ] Bathroom receptacle within 36 in of the basin and outside the tub/shower.
- [ ] Laundry, garage and outdoor receptacles meet their own rules.
- [ ] GFCI and AFCI marked on the panel schedule.
- [ ] Luminaires in damp/wet/insulated/fire-rated locations are listed.
- [ ] Dimmer or occupant sensor in habitable spaces.
- [ ] Panel working clearance is clear.

### Rough-in

- [ ] Boxes are set at the finish depth, plumb and secure.
- [ ] Box fill is adequate; smart devices fit.
- [ ] Cable is supported, protected and not damaged.
- [ ] Grounding is continuous and bonded at every box.
- [ ] Switch loops and three-way travellers are identified.
- [ ] Fire-rated and IC-rated fixtures are installed to the listing.
- [ ] Photos taken before insulation and drywall.

### Trim-out

- [ ] Every device and luminaire is the specified type and rating.
- [ ] GFCI and AFCI test and reset correctly.
- [ ] Dimming is smooth across the full range; no flicker or buzz.
- [ ] CCT and CRI match the schedule; no mixed bins in one view.
- [ ] Covers, gaskets and in-use covers are installed.
- [ ] Panel directory is complete and legible.
- [ ] Owner receives the schedule, cut sheets and warranty information.
