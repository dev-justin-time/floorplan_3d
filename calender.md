Phase 1: Pre-Construction & Site Prep (Weeks 1–3)
Before any physical building begins, the site must be evaluated and prepared.
Land Surveyor: Stakes out the exact property lines and the footprint of the house.
Geotechnical Engineer: Tests the soil to determine bearing capacity and informs the foundation design.
Excavator / Earthmover: Clears trees, grades the lot, and digs the trench for the foundation or the hole for a basement.
Utility Locator: Marks existing underground public utilities (gas, water, sewer, electric) to prevent accidental strikes.
Phase 2: Foundation & Substructure (Weeks 3–5)
Getting the home out of the ground and protected from moisture.
5. Formworkers (Carpenters): Build the wooden forms that will hold the wet concrete for footings and foundation walls.
6. Ironworkers / Rebar Installers: Place steel reinforcement bars (rebar) or mesh inside the forms for structural strength.
7. Plumber (Rough-in 1): Installs the underground sanitary sewer and water supply lines that pass through the foundation (must be done before the concrete pour and inspected).
10. Concrete Finishers: Pour, level, and smooth the concrete for footings, slab, or basement walls.
11. Waterproofing / Drainage Specialist: Applies waterproofing membranes to foundation walls and installs the perimeter French drain and sump pump.
Phase 3: Framing & Enclosure "Dried-In" (Weeks 5–9)
Creating the skeleton of the house and sealing it from the weather.
12. Framers (Rough Carpenters): Build the floor systems, exterior and interior walls, and the roof trusses/rafters.
13. Sheathing Crew: Nails structural plywood or OSB to the exterior walls and roof deck.
14. Roofers: Install underlayment, drip edge, and the final roofing material (shingles, metal, or tile).
15. Window & Exterior Door Installers: Install windows and exterior doors, and apply flashing and house wrap (Tyvek). (Once this is done, the house is considered "Dried-In," allowing interior work to begin regardless of weather).
Phase 4: Rough-Ins "The Guts" (Weeks 9–12)
Installing the hidden mechanical, electrical, and plumbing systems. Note: These trades must coordinate closely and require municipal inspections before walls are closed.
16. HVAC Technician (Rough-in): Installs the furnace, AC unit, and the large sheet-metal ductwork throughout the floors, walls, and ceilings. (Goes first because ducts are the bulkiest).
17. Plumber (Rough-in 2): Runs water supply lines (PEX or copper) and drain/waste/vent (DWV) pipes through the framed walls and floors.
18. Electrician (Rough-in): Runs electrical wires, installs breaker panels, and mounts boxes for outlets, switches, and light fixtures.
19. Low-Voltage / Smart Home Technician: Runs wires for security systems, speakers, networking, and structured wiring.
20. Building Inspector: Performs the "Rough-in Inspection" to approve all framing, plumbing, electrical, and HVAC before insulation is installed.
Phase 5: Insulation & Drywall (Weeks 12–15)
Sealing the home and creating smooth interior surfaces.
21. Insulation Installers: Install fiberglass batts, blown-in cellulose, or spray foam in the exterior walls, attic, and basement.
22. Drywall Hangers: Measure, cut, and screw large sheets of drywall to the studs and ceiling joists.
23. Drywall Finishers (Tapers/Mudders): Apply joint tape and multiple coats of "mud" (joint compound) to the seams, followed by extensive sanding to create smooth walls.
Phase 6: Interior Finishes "Trim-Out" (Weeks 15–20)
The house starts to look like a home. This phase requires careful sequencing to prevent damaging finished work.
24. Painters (First Coat): Apply primer and the first coat of paint to walls and ceilings (doing this before trim and floors prevents accidental spills).
25. Finish Carpenters: Install interior doors, baseboards, window casings, crown molding, and wainscoting.
26. Cabinet Installers: Hang kitchen, bathroom, and laundry room cabinets.
27. Countertop Templater: Comes in to laser-measure the installed cabinets for custom stone/quartz countertops.
28. Countertop Installers: Return a week later to install and seam the stone countertops.
29. Tile Setters: Install tile backsplashes, shower surrounds, and bathroom floors.
30. Flooring Installers: Install hardwood, laminate, or luxury vinyl plank (LVP). (Note: If using hardwood, it is sometimes installed before cabinets, but protected with heavy paper. Carpet is always installed last).
31. Painters (Final Coat): Return to paint the trim, doors, and apply the final topcoat to the walls, plus any touch-ups.
Phase 7: Final Mechanical Trim & Exterior (Weeks 20–23)
Connecting the final fixtures and finishing the outside of the house.
32. Electrician (Trim-out): Installs light fixtures, ceiling fans, outlet covers, and switch plates.
33. Plumber (Trim-out): Installs sinks, faucets, toilets, showers, and the water heater.
34. HVAC Technician (Trim-out): Installs vent registers, grilles, and thermostats, and performs final system balancing.
35. Appliance Delivery Crew: Delivers and hooks up the refrigerator, stove, dishwasher, and washer/dryer.
36. Siding / Exterior Cladding Crew: Installs brick, stone, vinyl, fiber cement, or stucco to the exterior walls.
37. Landscapers: Grade the final yard, lay sod or seed, plant trees/shrubs, and install irrigation systems.
38. Fence & Deck Builders: Construct outdoor living spaces and property boundaries.
39. Paving / Concrete Crew: Pour the driveway, walkways, and patios.
Phase 8: Final Clean & Closeout (Weeks 23–24)
Final Building Inspector: Performs the final walkthrough to issue the Certificate of Occupancy (CO).
Professional Cleaning Crew: Does a deep, construction-grade clean of the entire house (removing drywall dust, sticker residue, and debris).
Punch-List Crew: A small team (often the builder, finish carpenter, and painter) returns to fix minor issues: touching up a scuffed wall, adjusting a sticky door, or caulking a gap.
Real Estate Photographer: Takes photos for the listing (if it's a spec home).
Builder / Project Manager: Hands over the keys, warranties, and manuals to the homeowner.
💡 3 Pro-Tips About Construction Scheduling:
The "Critical Path": Certain trades hold up the entire project. If the framer is delayed by rain, the roofer, window crew, and all interior trades are subsequently delayed.
Inspection Bottlenecks: The schedule is entirely at the mercy of the local municipal inspector. If the inspector is booked for 3 days, no drywall can be hung until the rough-in is approved.
Material Lead Times: Custom windows, specialty cabinets, and stone countertops often have lead times of 4–8 weeks. Good project managers order these during the framing phase so they arrive exactly when needed.
